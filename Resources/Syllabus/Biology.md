# Biology (SL/HL)
Group 4 (Sciences) · first assessment 2025 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h · calculator allowed
Aliases: Biology, Bio, Biology HL, Biology SL

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1A: multiple choice and Paper 1B: data-based questions on experimental work, sat together | 1h30, 36% | 2h, 36% |
| Paper 2: Section A data-based and short-answer questions; Section B extended-response questions (a choice) | 1h30, 44% | 2h30, 44% |
| Internal assessment: scientific investigation (report up to 3000 words; research design, data analysis, conclusion, evaluation) | 20% | 20% |
Also the collaborative sciences project (required, not marked). Four themes (A to D) across four levels of organization (1 molecules, 2 cells, 3 organisms, 4 ecosystems): topic C3.2 is theme C, level 3. (HL) marks HL-only content.

## Topics ((HL) = additional higher level)
### A · Unity and diversity
- A1.1 Water: hydrogen bonding, cohesion, adhesion, solvent properties, thermal properties, water on Earth and the origin of life; (HL) physical properties for habitats
- A1.2 Nucleic acids: DNA and RNA structure, nucleotides, base pairing, the double helix, differences DNA vs RNA, DNA as the genetic material; (HL) Hershey–Chase, Chargaff's data, directionality (5′ to 3′)
- A2.1 Origins of cells (HL only): conditions on early Earth, Miller–Urey, spontaneous origin of cells, RNA as the first genetic material, LUCA, endosymbiosis evidence
- A2.2 Cell structure: cell theory, microscopy (light, electron, magnification and scale bars), prokaryote vs eukaryote, organelles, animal vs plant vs fungal cells, atypical cells, (HL) origin of eukaryotes by endosymbiosis, cell differentiation and multicellularity
- A2.3 Viruses (HL only): structure, diversity, lytic and lysogenic cycles, evidence for virus origins, rapid evolution in viruses
- A3.1 Diversity of organisms: species concepts, binomial nomenclature, variation, karyotypes and genome size, dichotomous keys, eDNA and barcodes
- A3.2 Classification and cladistics (HL only): taxonomic ranks, clades, cladograms from sequence data, molecular clocks, reclassification, the three domains
- A4.1 Evolution and speciation: evidence (fossils, homologous structures, DNA and protein sequences, selective breeding), convergent evolution, speciation by reproductive isolation (allopatric, sympatric), (HL) adaptive radiation, polyploidy, hybrids
- A4.2 Conservation of biodiversity: biodiversity at ecosystem, species and genetic level, current extinction crisis, causes (habitat loss, invasive species, pollution, overexploitation), in situ and ex situ conservation, EDGE species

### B · Form and function
- B1.1 Carbohydrates and lipids: condensation and hydrolysis, monosaccharides, disaccharides, polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose), glycoproteins, triglycerides and phospholipids, saturated vs unsaturated fats, steroids
- B1.2 Proteins: amino acids, peptide bonds, primary to quaternary structure, effect of pH and temperature, denaturation, (HL) fibrous vs globular, conjugated proteins, R-group interactions
- B2.1 Membranes and membrane transport: fluid mosaic model, diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion, channel and pump proteins, active transport, endocytosis and exocytosis, (HL) fluidity, cholesterol, membrane potentials, cell adhesion
- B2.2 Organelles and compartmentalization: the nucleus and its envelope, ribosomes, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, mitochondria and chloroplasts adapted to function, (HL) cell fractionation
- B2.3 Cell specialization: stem cells and differentiation, cell size and surface area to volume ratio, specialized cells (red blood cells, neurons, sperm and egg, alveolar cells), (HL) adaptations of striated muscle fibres
- B3.1 Gas exchange: properties of gas-exchange surfaces, lungs and ventilation, lung volumes and spirometry, leaves and stomata, transpiration, (HL) haemoglobin, oxygen dissociation curves, Bohr shift
- B3.2 Transport: arteries, veins and capillaries, the heart and cardiac cycle, xylem and phloem, transpiration stream, (HL) tissue fluid and lymph, translocation in phloem, root pressure
- B3.3 Muscle and motility (HL only): sliding filament model, sarcomere, antagonistic muscles, joints, locomotion in animals
- B4.1 Adaptation to environment: habitats, adaptations to extreme conditions (xerophytes, halophytes), biomes and climate (temperature and rainfall graphs)
- B4.2 Ecological niches: niche concept, modes of nutrition (autotrophs, heterotrophs, saprotrophs, mixotrophs), adaptations of herbivores and predators, competitive exclusion, (HL) fundamental vs realized niche

### C · Interaction and interdependence
- C1.1 Enzymes and metabolism: active site, induced fit, activation energy, factors affecting activity (temperature, pH, substrate concentration), measuring rate, immobilized enzymes, (HL) competitive and non-competitive inhibition, end-product inhibition, mechanism-based inhibition
- C1.2 Cell respiration: ATP, aerobic vs anaerobic respiration, respirometers, (HL) glycolysis, link reaction, Krebs cycle, electron transport chain, chemiosmosis, oxidative phosphorylation, respiratory quotient, lipids and amino acids as substrates
- C1.3 Photosynthesis: light absorption by pigments, chromatography of pigments, absorption and action spectra, limiting factors, carbon dioxide enrichment, (HL) light-dependent reactions (photosystems, photolysis), light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle, RuBisCo)
- C2.1 Chemical signalling (HL only): receptors, ligands, hormones, neurotransmitters, cytokines, signal transduction, second messengers, transmembrane and intracellular receptors
- C2.2 Neural signalling: neurons, resting and action potentials, myelination and saltatory conduction, synapses and neurotransmitters, (HL) effects of drugs and pain signalling, oscilloscope traces
- C3.1 Integration of body systems: nervous and endocrine coordination, reflex arcs, the brain, heart-rate control, feedback, (HL) plant hormones and tropisms (auxin)
- C3.2 Defence against disease: pathogens, skin and mucous membranes, blood clotting, innate vs adaptive immunity, phagocytes, lymphocytes, antibodies, vaccination, herd immunity, zoonoses, antibiotics and resistance, HIV
- C4.1 Populations and communities: population size estimation (quadrats, capture–mark–release–recapture, Lincoln index), carrying capacity, logistic growth, interspecific relationships (competition, predation, mutualism, parasitism), keystone species, (HL) chi-squared test for association
- C4.2 Transfers of energy and matter: ecosystems as open systems, food chains and webs, trophic levels, pyramids of energy, energy loss between levels, primary production, the carbon cycle, (HL) gross and net productivity, nutrient cycling

### D · Continuity and change
- D1.1 DNA replication: semi-conservative replication, helicase, DNA polymerases, PCR and gel electrophoresis, (HL) leading and lagging strands, Okazaki fragments, proofreading
- D1.2 Protein synthesis: transcription, translation, the genetic code, codons and anticodons, ribosomes, (HL) promoters, post-transcriptional modification, splicing, post-translational modification
- D1.3 Mutation and gene editing: point mutations (substitution, insertion, deletion), mutagens, consequences, (HL) gene knockout, CRISPR-Cas9, conserved and highly conserved sequences
- D2.1 Cell and nuclear division: mitosis and meiosis compared, cell cycle, chromosome condensation, cancer and oncogenes, (HL) cyclins, mitotic index
- D2.2 Gene expression (HL only): regulation of transcription, epigenetics, methylation, environment and gene expression, monozygotic twin studies
- D2.3 Water potential: solute and pressure potential, plasmolysis, turgor, hypotonic and hypertonic solutions in cells and tissues
- D3.1 Reproduction: sexual vs asexual, gametes and fertilization, the menstrual cycle and its hormones, IVF, sexual reproduction in flowering plants (pollination, fertilization, seed dispersal), (HL) spermatogenesis and oogenesis, hCG and pregnancy tests
- D3.2 Inheritance: genotype and phenotype, dominant, recessive, codominant alleles, Punnett grids, multiple alleles (ABO), sex linkage, pedigree charts, polygenic traits, (HL) dihybrid crosses, linkage, chi-squared test on ratios
- D3.3 Homeostasis: negative feedback, blood glucose (insulin, glucagon) and diabetes, thermoregulation, (HL) osmoregulation and the kidney, nephron structure, ADH
- D4.1 Natural selection: variation, overproduction, competition, survival and reproduction, sexual selection, (HL) gene pools, allele frequencies, Hardy–Weinberg, artificial selection
- D4.2 Stability and change: sustainability of ecosystems, tipping points, monocultures, eutrophication, biomagnification, plastic pollution, rewilding, (HL) ecological succession
- D4.3 Climate change: anthropogenic causes, positive feedback (melting ice, permafrost), effects on ecosystems (range shifts, phenology, coral bleaching), carbon sequestration

## Skills and tools that get examined
- Data analysis: reading graphs, percentage change, mean and standard deviation, error bars, correlation vs causation, t-test and chi-squared (HL) where data allow.
- Drawing and annotating: cells, organelles, the heart, a nephron, diagrams with labels and scale.
- Experimental design: independent, dependent and controlled variables; control groups; sample size; ethics.

## How to quiz it
- Paper 1A style: four-option multiple choice on one idea; ask for the letter and why.
- Paper 1B style: a short experiment with data; ask them to describe the trend, calculate a percentage change and evaluate the conclusion.
- Paper 2 Section A: data-based questions from a real-looking study. Section B: extended responses (4 to 8 marks per part) such as "Describe the process of…", "Explain how…", with marks for each correct, distinct point and up to 1 mark for clarity.
- Mark point by point: each correct idea earns a mark up to the total; reward precise terms (e.g. "active site", "complementary", "semi-conservative", "against the concentration gradient using ATP").
- Common slips to probe: "enzymes are killed" instead of denatured, confusing transcription and translation, mixing up mitosis and meiosis outcomes, osmosis described as solute movement, correlation stated as cause.
