# Business management (SL/HL)
Group 3 (Individuals and societies) · first assessment 2024 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h
Aliases: BM, Business, Business Mgmt, Business Management HL

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: unseen case study built on a pre-released statement | 1h30, 35% | 1h30, 25% |
| Paper 2: unseen stimulus with a quantitative focus | 1h30, 35% | 1h45, 30% |
| Paper 3: unseen stimulus about a social enterprise; ends with a 17-mark "recommend a plan of action" (HL only) | n/a | 1h15, 25% |
| Business research project (IA): a real business issue through one key concept | 30% | 20% |
Four key concepts: change, creativity, ethics, sustainability. HL rewards AO3: evaluating and recommending between competing strategic options.

## Topics ((HL) = HL only)
### 1 · Introduction to business management
- 1.1 What a business is: inputs, outputs, sectors (primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary), sectoral change, entrepreneurship and intrapreneurship, reasons for starting up and problems start-ups face
- 1.2 Types of business entities: sole traders, partnerships, privately and publicly held companies, for-profit social enterprises, non-profit social enterprises (NGOs, charities), cooperatives
- 1.3 Business objectives: vision and mission statements, aims, strategic/tactical/operational objectives, corporate social responsibility (CSR) and its changing role
- 1.4 Stakeholders: internal and external, their interests, conflicts between them, stakeholder mapping
- 1.5 Growth and evolution: economies and diseconomies of scale, small vs large, internal (organic) vs external growth (mergers, acquisitions, takeovers, joint ventures, strategic alliances, franchising)
- 1.6 Multinational companies (MNCs): reasons for growth, impact on host countries

### 2 · Human resource management
- 2.1 Introduction: HR planning, labour turnover (calculation), internal and external factors, recruitment, training (on/off the job, induction), appraisal (formative, summative, 360°, self), dismissal and redundancy, changing work patterns (gig economy, remote work, flexitime, outsourcing, offshoring, reshoring)
- 2.2 Organizational structure: delegation, span of control, levels of hierarchy, chain of command, bureaucracy, centralization/decentralization, delayering, flat/tall/matrix structures, structures by product, function, region; project-based organization
- 2.3 Leadership and management: functions of management, leadership styles (autocratic, paternalistic, democratic, laissez-faire, situational), management vs leadership
- 2.4 Motivation and demotivation: Taylor, Maslow, Herzberg, McClelland, Deci and Ryan's self-determination theory, equity theory and expectancy theory (Adams, Vroom); financial rewards (salary, wages, commission, profit-related pay, performance-related pay, employee share ownership, fringe payments) and non-financial (job enrichment, rotation, empowerment, purpose, teamwork)
- 2.5 (HL) Organizational (corporate) culture: types, culture clashes in growth and mergers, individuals influencing culture
- 2.6 Communication: formal and informal, barriers to communication
- 2.7 (HL) Industrial/employee relations: sources of conflict, approaches by employees (collective bargaining, industrial action) and employers, conflict resolution

### 3 · Finance and accounts
- 3.1 Introduction: role of finance, capital vs revenue expenditure
- 3.2 Sources of finance: internal (personal funds, retained profit, sale of assets) and external (share capital, loan capital, overdrafts, trade credit, crowdfunding, leasing, microfinance, business angels)
- 3.3 Costs and revenues: fixed, variable, semi-variable, direct, indirect/overheads; total revenue and revenue streams
- 3.4 Final accounts: purpose to stakeholders, profit and loss account (income statement), balance sheet (statement of financial position), intangible assets; (HL) depreciation (straight line and units of production)
- 3.5 Profitability and liquidity ratio analysis: gross profit margin, profit margin, ROCE, current ratio, acid-test ratio; strategies to improve them
- 3.6 (HL) Efficiency ratio analysis: inventory turnover, debtor days, creditor days, gearing ratio
- 3.7 Cash flow: profit vs cash flow, working capital, cash-flow forecasts, liquidity position, strategies for cash-flow problems
- 3.8 Investment appraisal: payback period, average rate of return (ARR); (HL) net present value (NPV) with discount factors
- 3.9 (HL) Budgets: importance, cost and profit centres, variance analysis (favourable/adverse)

### 4 · Marketing
- 4.1 Introduction: market orientation vs product orientation, commercial vs social marketing, market share and market growth calculations, importance of market share and leadership
- 4.2 Marketing planning: the marketing mix, segmentation, targeting, positioning (perception maps), unique selling point (USP), differentiation
- 4.3 (HL) Sales forecasting: moving averages, trends, seasonal and cyclical variation, benefits and limitations
- 4.4 Market research: primary and secondary, qualitative vs quantitative, sampling methods (quota, random, convenience, cluster, snowballing, stratified), ethics
- 4.5 The seven Ps: product (life cycle, extension strategies, BCG matrix, branding, packaging), price (cost-plus, penetration, skimming, psychological, loss leader, price discrimination, dynamic, competitive, contribution), promotion (above/below the line, promotional mix, social media marketing), place (distribution channels), people, process, physical evidence
- 4.6 (HL) International marketing: entering new markets, opportunities and threats, cultural differences, globalization

### 5 · Operations management
- 5.1 Introduction: role of operations, operations and sustainability (economic, social, environmental)
- 5.2 Operations methods: job, batch, mass/flow, mass customization; choosing a method
- 5.3 (HL) Lean production and quality management: kaizen, just-in-time, kanban, andon; cradle to cradle; quality control vs quality assurance, TQM, national and international quality standards
- 5.4 Location: factors (qualitative and quantitative), reorganizing production (outsourcing, offshoring, insourcing, reshoring)
- 5.5 Break-even analysis: contribution (unit and total), break-even chart and quantity, margin of safety, target profit and target price, effects of changes, limitations
- 5.6 (HL) Production planning: local and global supply chains, just-in-time vs just-in-case, stock control charts (buffer stock, reorder level and quantity, lead time), capacity utilization rate, defect rate, productivity rates (labour, capital, operating), cost to buy vs cost to make
- 5.7 (HL) Crisis management and contingency planning
- 5.8 (HL) Research and development: importance, intellectual property protection (patents, copyright, trademarks), innovation (product, process, positioning, paradigm), adaptive vs innovative creativity, pace of change
- 5.9 (HL) Management information systems: data analytics, databases, cybersecurity and cybercrime, critical infrastructure, artificial intelligence, big data, customer loyalty programmes, digital Taylorism, data mining, virtual reality, the Internet of Things, ethical issues

### Business management toolkit (used across Papers 1–3)
SWOT, Ansoff matrix, STEEPLE, Boston Consulting Group matrix, business plan, decision trees, descriptive statistics, circular business models; (HL) Gantt charts, force field analysis, Porter's generic strategies, Hofstede's cultural dimensions, critical path analysis, contribution-based decisions, simple linear regression.

## How to quiz it
- Definitions ("Define the term delayering" [2 marks]) and short explanations ("Explain two advantages of…" [4 marks]).
- Calculations: break-even, margin of safety, ratios, NPV and payback, labour turnover, capacity utilization, variances, stock control.
- Application: give a short made-up case (a bubble-tea start-up in Shenzhen, a social enterprise selling recycled uniforms) and ask them to apply a toolkit model by name.
- 10-mark "Discuss/evaluate" answers: both sides applied to the case, then a justified conclusion. HL Paper 3: recommend a plan of action for a social enterprise using human needs, ethics and sustainability.
