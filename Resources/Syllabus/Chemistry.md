# Chemistry (SL/HL)
Group 4 (Sciences) · first assessment 2025 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h · calculator on both papers, data booklet provided
Aliases: Chemistry, Chem, Chemistry HL, Chemistry SL

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1A: multiple choice and Paper 1B: data-based questions on experimental work, sat together | 1h30, 36% | 2h, 36% |
| Paper 2: short-answer and extended-response questions across the syllabus | 1h30, 44% | 2h30, 44% |
| Internal assessment: scientific investigation (report up to 3000 words; research design, data analysis, conclusion, evaluation) | 20% | 20% |
Also the collaborative sciences project (required, not marked). The course is two strands, Structure and Reactivity, each split into topics; (HL) marks HL-only content. Answers with units, state symbols where asked, and sensible significant figures.

## Topics ((HL) = additional higher level)
### Structure 1 · Models of the particulate nature of matter
- S1.1 Introduction to the particulate nature of matter: elements, compounds, mixtures, separation techniques, kinetic molecular theory, states and changes of state, kelvin temperature
- S1.2 The nuclear atom: protons, neutrons, electrons, isotopes, relative atomic mass from abundances, mass spectra; (HL) interpreting mass spectra
- S1.3 Electron configurations: emission spectra, hydrogen spectrum and energy levels, convergence, sublevels and orbitals (s, p, d, f), Aufbau, Hund's rule, Pauli principle, full and condensed configurations; (HL) ionization energies, trends and evidence for sublevels, calculating ionization energy from the convergence limit
- S1.4 Counting particles by mass: the mole, Avogadro's constant, molar mass, empirical and molecular formulas, percentage composition, concentration (mol dm⁻³, g dm⁻³), Avogadro's law
- S1.5 Ideal gases: assumptions of the ideal gas model, deviations of real gases, pV = nRT, molar volume, combined gas law

### Structure 2 · Models of bonding and structure
- S2.1 The ionic model: ions and their charges, ionic bonding, lattice structure, properties of ionic compounds, naming and formulas including polyatomic ions; (HL) lattice enthalpy and the factors affecting it
- S2.2 The covalent model: single, double, triple bonds, coordination bonds, Lewis structures, VSEPR shapes and bond angles, bond polarity and electronegativity, molecular polarity, giant covalent structures (diamond, graphite, graphene, SiO₂), intermolecular forces (London, dipole–dipole, hydrogen bonding), chromatography; (HL) resonance, benzene, expanded octets, formal charge, sigma and pi bonds, hybridization (sp, sp², sp³)
- S2.3 The metallic model: metallic bonding, properties of metals, trends in melting point, (HL) transition elements and their properties
- S2.4 From models to materials: bonding triangle (ionic, covalent, metallic character), alloys, polymers, addition and condensation polymerization, plastics and their impact

### Structure 3 · Classification of matter
- S3.1 The periodic table: periods, groups, blocks, periodicity of atomic radius, ionic radius, ionization energy, electron affinity, electronegativity, metallic character, oxides (acidic, basic, amphoteric), oxidation states, group 1 and group 17 reactions; (HL) discontinuities in ionization energy, transition element properties (variable oxidation states, coloured complexes from d-orbital splitting, catalysis, magnetism)
- S3.2 Functional groups: homologous series, structural, condensed and skeletal formulas, IUPAC naming (up to six carbons), functional groups (alkane, alkene, alkyne, halogenoalkane, alcohol, aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid, ester, ether, amine, amide), structural isomers, primary/secondary/tertiary; (HL) stereoisomers (cis–trans, E/Z, optical isomers and chiral centres), mass spectrometry fragments, infrared spectra, ¹H NMR (chemical shift, integration, splitting), index of hydrogen deficiency

### Reactivity 1 · What drives chemical reactions?
- R1.1 Measuring enthalpy changes: exothermic and endothermic, energy profiles, calorimetry Q = mcΔT, enthalpy change per mole, sources of error in calorimetry
- R1.2 Energy cycles in reactions: bond enthalpies and their limits, Hess's law, standard enthalpies of formation and combustion; (HL) Born–Haber cycles
- R1.3 Energy from fuels: complete and incomplete combustion, fossil fuels and their carbon emissions, biofuels, fuel cells, specific energy and energy density
- R1.4 Entropy and spontaneity (HL only): entropy S, predicting ΔS, Gibbs energy ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, spontaneity and temperature, ΔG and the equilibrium constant ΔG° = −RT ln K

### Reactivity 2 · How much, how fast and how far?
- R2.1 How much? The amount of chemical change: balanced equations, mole ratios, limiting and excess reactants, theoretical, experimental and percentage yield, atom economy, titration calculations
- R2.2 How fast? The rate of chemical change: collision theory, measuring rate, factors (concentration, temperature, surface area, catalysts), Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution, activation energy; (HL) rate equations, orders of reaction, rate constant k and its units, rate-determining step and mechanisms, Arrhenius equation and plots
- R2.3 How far? The extent of chemical change: dynamic equilibrium, the equilibrium constant K and its expression, Le Chatelier's principle, effect of conditions on position and on K, the reaction quotient Q; (HL) calculating K from equilibrium amounts (ICE tables)

### Reactivity 3 · What are the mechanisms of chemical change?
- R3.1 Proton transfer reactions: Brønsted–Lowry acids and bases, conjugate pairs, amphiprotic species, pH and [H⁺], strong vs weak acids and bases, neutralization, pH curves; (HL) Kw and temperature, pOH, Ka, Kb, pKa, pKb, buffers and their pH, salt hydrolysis, indicators and choosing one, Lewis acids and bases
- R3.2 Electron transfer reactions: oxidation states, redox half-equations, oxidizing and reducing agents, reactivity series, primary cells and electrolytic cells, oxidation of alcohols, reduction of carbonyls; (HL) standard electrode potentials, standard hydrogen electrode, E°cell and ΔG° = −nFE°, electrolysis of aqueous solutions, electroplating
- R3.3 Electron sharing reactions: radicals, homolytic fission, free-radical substitution of alkanes (initiation, propagation, termination)
- R3.4 Electron-pair sharing reactions: nucleophiles and electrophiles, nucleophilic substitution, electrophilic addition to alkenes, heterolytic fission; (HL) SN1 vs SN2 mechanisms with curly arrows, Markovnikov addition, electrophilic substitution of benzene, complex ion formation as Lewis acid–base reactions

## Skills and tools that get examined
- Uncertainties and errors in measurements, propagation, percentage error vs literature value; graphs of rate and concentration data.
- Writing equations with state symbols; half-equations; curly-arrow mechanisms (HL).
- Using the data booklet: bond enthalpies, electronegativities, pKa values, IR and NMR data.

## How to quiz it
- Paper 1A style: four-option multiple choice on one concept or a short calculation; ask for the letter and the reason.
- Paper 1B style: a practical with data (a titration, a calorimetry run, a rate experiment); ask them to process the data, state an uncertainty and suggest an improvement.
- Paper 2 style: a context in parts; "deduce", "outline", "explain", "calculate", "draw the Lewis structure", "sketch the energy profile".
- Calculations: mark the mole ratio and units; give method marks for the right approach, follow-through for a carried error; answers to the s.f. of the data.
- Explanations: look for the precise model language (e.g. "greater nuclear charge, same shielding", "more frequent successful collisions with energy ≥ Ea", "equilibrium shifts to oppose the change").
- Common slips to probe: confusing bond breaking (endothermic) with making, ΔH sign in calorimetry, forgetting to convert cm³ to dm³, using Kc with solids, pH of a weak acid treated as strong, curly arrows from atoms instead of bonds or lone pairs.
