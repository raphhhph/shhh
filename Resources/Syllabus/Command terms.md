# IB command terms and how answers are marked
Use these when writing questions and when marking. The command term sets how deep the answer must go.

## Objective 1: knowing
- Define: give the precise meaning (exact definition, as in the subject glossary). 1–2 marks.
- State / List / Identify: give a name, value or brief answer, no explanation.
- Label / Draw / Sketch: diagram work. Draw = accurate, labelled; sketch = general shape and key features.

## Objective 2: understanding and applying
- Outline: a brief account or summary. Usually 2–4 marks.
- Describe: a detailed account (what happens, not why).
- Explain: a detailed account including reasons or causes: "because…, so…, which leads to…". Marks for each linked step.
- Distinguish: make the differences clear.
- Calculate / Determine / Find: obtain a numerical answer showing working. Method marks then accuracy marks.
- Apply / Use: use knowledge in a given context (the case, the data, the text).
- Suggest: propose a solution or hypothesis; there may be more than one plausible answer.
- Comment: give a judgment based on a calculation or statement.

## Objective 3: analysing, evaluating, synthesising
- Analyse: break down to bring out the essential elements and how they relate.
- Compare / Contrast / Compare and contrast: similarities, differences, or both, always referring to both items.
- Examine: consider an argument or concept to reveal its assumptions and interrelationships.
- Discuss: a considered and balanced review with a range of arguments, factors or hypotheses; a conclusion supported by evidence.
- Evaluate: weigh strengths and limitations, make an appraisal: needs a justified judgment.
- To what extent: consider the merits of an argument or concept; a conclusion with a clear "how far".
- Justify: give valid reasons or evidence for an answer or conclusion.
- Recommend: present an advisable course of action with justification (Business Paper 3, Economics Paper 3).

## Marking like an examiner
- Short questions: point-based. Each correct, relevant point or linked step earns a mark; repetition earns nothing.
- Long answers (essays, 10–15+ marks): markbands. Top band = accurate knowledge, terms used precisely, applied to the context, balanced evaluation, real examples, a justified conclusion. Middle band = mostly description, some application, one-sided evaluation. Low band = general or inaccurate, no application.
- Calculations: method mark even if the arithmetic slips; follow-through for a later part that correctly uses an earlier wrong answer; units and rounding as asked.
- Diagrams: correctly labelled axes, curves and equilibria, shift shown with arrows, the relevant area shaded; a diagram must be explained in the text to earn full marks.
- When giving feedback: the mark, what earned it, what was missing (be specific), and a short model answer.
