# Economics (SL/HL)
Group 3 (Individuals and societies) · first assessment 2022 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h
Aliases: Econ, Economics HL, Economics SL

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: extended response, (a) explain 10 marks + (b) evaluate with real-world examples 15 marks | 1h15, 30% | 1h15, 20% |
| Paper 2: data response on a text with data (definitions, diagrams, calculations at HL, a 15-mark evaluation) | 1h45, 40% | 1h45, 30% |
| Paper 3: policy paper, quantitative, ends with a 10-mark policy recommendation (HL only) | n/a | 1h45, 30% |
| Internal assessment: portfolio of three commentaries on news articles, each a different unit and key concept | 30% | 20% |
Nine key concepts thread everything: scarcity, choice, efficiency, equity, economic well-being, sustainability, change, interdependence, intervention.

## Topics ((HL) = HL-only content inside a topic; whole HL-only topics are marked)
### 1 · Introduction to economics
- 1.1 What is economics: scarcity, choice, opportunity cost, free and economic goods, PPC (shifts, points inside/on/outside), circular flow of income, factors of production
- 1.2 How economists approach the world: positive vs normative, ceteris paribus, models, rational and behavioural economics, economic methodology, history of economic thought (Smith, Marx, Keynes, Hayek, Friedman, Raworth's doughnut)

### 2 · Microeconomics
- 2.1 Demand: law of demand, non-price determinants, movements vs shifts, (HL) linear demand functions Qd = a − bP, plotting and shifting them
- 2.2 Supply: law of supply, non-price determinants, (HL) linear supply functions Qs = c + dP
- 2.3 Competitive market equilibrium: excess demand/supply, price mechanism (signalling, incentive, rationing), consumer and producer surplus, allocative efficiency, (HL) calculating equilibrium and surplus areas from functions
- 2.4 Critique of maximizing behaviour: behavioural economics (bounded rationality, self-control, selfishness, imperfect information), biases (anchoring, availability, framing, default choices), nudges, choice architecture; business objectives beyond profit
- 2.5 Elasticity of demand: PED (calculation, determinants, along a linear curve, total revenue, primary vs manufactured goods), YED (normal, inferior, luxury; sector change), XED
- 2.6 Elasticity of supply: PES, determinants, primary vs manufactured goods
- 2.7 Role of government in microeconomics: price controls (ceilings, floors, minimum wage), indirect taxes (specific and ad valorem), subsidies, direct provision, command and control, consumer nudges; (HL) calculating effects on stakeholders, tax incidence, government revenue/spending, welfare loss
- 2.8 Market failure: externalities (negative/positive, production/consumption; MPB, MSB, MPC, MSC diagrams; welfare loss) and common pool resources (tragedy of the commons, sustainability); policies (Pigouvian tax, carbon tax, tradable permits, legislation, education, collective self-governance)
- 2.9 Market failure: public goods (non-rival, non-excludable, free rider problem, government provision)
- 2.10 Market failure: asymmetric information (adverse selection, moral hazard; screening, signalling, regulation) (HL)
- 2.11 Market failure: market power (HL only): perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly (collusive and non-collusive, game theory, kinked demand), monopolistic competition; costs and revenues, profit maximization (MC = MR), natural monopoly, regulation and anti-monopoly policy
- 2.12 The market's inability to achieve equity

### 3 · Macroeconomics
- 3.1 Measuring economic activity: GDP, GNI, GNP, nominal vs real, per capita, PPP; the business cycle; alternative measures of well-being (OECD Better Life Index, Happiness Index, HPI); (HL) calculating real GDP with a deflator
- 3.2 Aggregate demand and aggregate supply: components of AD (C + I + G + X − M) and their determinants; SRAS; LRAS in the Keynesian and new classical (monetarist) views; equilibrium, recessionary and inflationary gaps
- 3.3 Macroeconomic objectives: economic growth (causes, consequences); low unemployment (measurement, types: structural, frictional, seasonal, cyclical; costs; natural rate); low and stable inflation (CPI, deflation, demand-pull, cost-push, costs); (HL) calculating unemployment and inflation rates, weighted price index, short- and long-run Phillips curve; sustainable government debt
- 3.4 Economics of inequality and poverty: Lorenz curve, Gini coefficient, absolute vs relative poverty, multidimensional poverty, causes, policies (progressive taxes, transfer payments, minimum wage, universal basic services); (HL) calculating marginal and average tax rates
- 3.5 Monetary policy: central bank goals, interest rates, open market operations, reserve requirements, quantitative easing, effectiveness and constraints; (HL) demand and supply of money, money creation by commercial banks, real interest rates
- 3.6 Fiscal policy: government revenue and spending, automatic stabilizers, discretionary policy, crowding out, budget deficits/surpluses; (HL) the Keynesian multiplier (calculating with MPC, MPS, MPT, MPM)
- 3.7 Supply-side policies: market-based (labour market reform, competition, incentives) and interventionist (education, infrastructure, R&D, industrial policy)

### 4 · The global economy
- 4.1 Benefits of international trade; (HL) absolute and comparative advantage with calculations, limitations of the theory
- 4.2 Types of trade protection: tariffs, quotas, production subsidies, export subsidies, administrative barriers; (HL) calculating effects on revenue, consumers, producers and welfare
- 4.3 Arguments for and against trade protection (infant industry, national security, anti-dumping, protecting jobs vs retaliation, inefficiency, higher prices)
- 4.4 Economic integration: preferential trade agreements, bilateral/multilateral, free trade areas, customs unions, common markets, monetary union (pros and cons), the WTO
- 4.5 Exchange rates: floating, fixed and managed systems, appreciation/depreciation, devaluation/revaluation, causes and consequences; (HL) calculating exchange-rate conversions
- 4.6 Balance of payments: current, capital and financial accounts; current account deficits and surpluses, their correction; (HL) Marshall–Lerner condition and J-curve, calculating BoP components
- 4.7 Sustainable development: the Sustainable Development Goals, relationship with poverty
- 4.8 Measuring development: single indicators (GDP/GNI per capita, health, education) and composite indicators (HDI, IHDI, GII, MPI, Happy Planet Index)
- 4.9 Barriers to growth and development: poverty traps, institutional factors, inequality, geography, debt, (HL) terms of trade calculations and long-term decline
- 4.10 Growth and development strategies: trade, diversification, market-led vs interventionist, aid, FDI and MNCs, microfinance, fair trade, women's empowerment, good governance

## How to quiz it
- Definitions are free marks: ask for exact definitions (match the IB glossary when the user has it).
- Diagrams: ask them to describe the axes, curves, labels and the shift ("Draw a diagram to show…"); check labels P, Q, equilibrium points and the welfare loss area.
- Paper 1 (a) 10 marks: explain with a diagram. Paper 1 (b) 15 marks: evaluate with real-world examples, consider stakeholders, short vs long run, a justified conclusion.
- Paper 2: short (2–4 mark) definitions and calculations from data, then a 15-mark "using the text/data and your knowledge, evaluate…".
- Paper 3 (HL): calculations (PED, tax incidence, multiplier, exchange rates, Gini-style comparisons, terms of trade) on a country case, ending with "recommend a policy" (10 marks): a clear recommendation, backed by the data, with trade-offs.
- Evaluation tools to look for: stakeholders, short vs long run, priorities, assumptions, effectiveness, unintended consequences, real-world examples.
