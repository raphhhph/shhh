# English A: Language and literature (SL/HL)
Group 1 (Studies in language and literature) · first assessment 2021 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h
Aliases: English A, English Lang and Lit, Lang & Lit, L&L, English A L&L

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: guided textual analysis of unseen non-literary text(s), with a guiding question | 1h15, one text, 35% | 2h15, two texts, 35% |
| Paper 2: comparative essay on two literary works studied, one question from four | 1h45, 35% | 1h45, 25% |
| Individual oral (IA): 10 min + 5 min questions; how one literary work and one non-literary body of work present a global issue | 30% | 20% |
| HL essay: 1,200–1,500 words on one work or body of work (HL only) | n/a | 20% |
SL studies four literary works; HL six. Paper 1 is marked on: understanding and interpretation; analysis and evaluation; focus and organization; language.

## Areas of exploration and concepts
- Readers, writers and texts: how language makes meaning; craft and effect; the reader's role
- Time and space: how context (historical, cultural, social, geographic) shapes a text and its reception
- Intertextuality, connecting texts: genre, convention, influence, comparison
Seven concepts: identity, culture, creativity, communication, perspective, transformation, representation.

## Global issue fields of inquiry (for the individual oral)
Culture, identity and community · Beliefs, values and education · Politics, power and justice · Art, creativity and the imagination · Science, technology and the environment.

## Non-literary text types and their tools
- Advertisements and posters: target audience, imperatives, slogans, imagery, colour, layout, call to action
- Cartoons, comics and graphic novels: panels, gutters, framing, speech balloons, captions, symbolism, colour palette, visual metaphor, juxtaposition
- Speeches: rhetorical devices (anaphora, tricolon, rhetorical questions, inclusive pronouns), ethos/pathos/logos, register
- Opinion pieces, editorials, blogs: tone, bias, persona, anecdote, statistics, structure of argument
- Infographics: data selection, hierarchy, icons, colour coding, what's left out
- Photographs and film stills: composition, angle, focus, lighting, rule of thirds
- Social media posts, websites, letters, interviews, memoirs, travel writing, manifestos

## Literary analysis vocabulary
Narrative voice and perspective, characterization, setting, structure (chronology, flashback, framing), imagery and symbolism, motif, tone and mood, diction, syntax, irony, juxtaposition, foreshadowing, dialogue; for poetry: form, meter, enjambment, caesura, sound devices; for drama: stage directions, dramatic irony, soliloquy.

## How to quiz it
- Give a short unseen extract (an ad's copy, a speech excerpt, a description of a cartoon) and a guiding question; ask for a thesis and one analytical paragraph: technique, evidence, effect on the reader, link to the guiding question. Mark on the four criteria.
- Technique drills: "Name the technique and its effect" for short quotes.
- Paper 2 practice: give a question ("How do two works you have studied present conflict between individuals and society?") and ask for a comparative thesis and paragraph plan using their actual works (read their notes for which works they study).
- Individual oral prep: ask them to choose a global issue for a pairing and outline the 10 minutes.
