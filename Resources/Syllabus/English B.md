# Language B: English (SL/HL)
Group 2 (Language acquisition) · first assessment 2020 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h
Aliases: English B, English B SL, English B HL, Eng B

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 · Productive skills: one writing task from a choice of three, each in a stated text type | 1h15, 250–400 words, 25% | 1h30, 450–600 words, 25% |
| Paper 2 · Receptive skills: listening (three audio texts, 45 min at SL, 1h at HL) and reading (three written texts, 1h) | 1h45 total, 50% | 2h total, 50% |
| Individual oral (IA): SL a visual stimulus linked to a theme; HL an extract from one of the two literary works studied; then conversation on the theme and other themes | 12–15 min plus 15 min preparation, 25% | 12–15 min plus 20 min preparation, 25% |
Paper 1 is marked on language (range, accuracy), message (ideas, development, relevance) and conceptual understanding (text type, register, audience, purpose). HL reads two works of literature in English.

## Five themes and recommended topics
- Identities: lifestyles, health and well-being, beliefs and values, subcultures, language and identity
- Experiences: leisure activities, holidays and travel, life stories, rites of passage, customs and traditions, migration
- Human ingenuity: entertainment, artistic expressions, communication and media, technology, scientific innovation
- Social organization: social relationships, community, social engagement, education, the working world, law and order
- Sharing the planet: the environment, human rights, peace and conflict, equality, globalization, ethics, urban and rural environment

## Text types for Paper 1 and their conventions
- Personal: diary (date, first person, reflective, informal), blog (title, date, engaging opening, direct address, invites comments), personal letter or email (greeting, informal register, closing, name)
- Professional: formal letter (addresses, date, "Dear Sir or Madam / Dear Ms X", "Yours faithfully / sincerely"), report (title, headings, findings, recommendations, impersonal tone), proposal (aims, plan, benefits), official email, set of instructions or guidelines (imperatives, numbered steps)
- Mass media: article (headline, by-line, subheadings, paragraphs, quotes), speech (salutation, rhetorical questions, signposting, call to action, thanks), brochure or leaflet (headings, bullet points, persuasive), interview (introduction, Q&A layout), review (title, rating, opinion with reasons), news report (5 Ws in the lead, third person), opinion column, social media post

## Language worth drilling
Tenses and their uses (present perfect vs past simple, past perfect, future forms), conditionals (zero to third, mixed), passive voice, reported speech, relative clauses, modal verbs (obligation, possibility, deduction), gerund vs infinitive, articles, prepositions, phrasal verbs, linking devices (however, whereas, furthermore, consequently, nevertheless), hedging (it could be argued that…), register shifts (formal vs informal vocabulary), idioms by theme, collocations, word formation (prefixes, suffixes).

## How to quiz it
- Vocabulary by theme: a definition, ask for the word; or a word, ask for a sentence in context; or synonyms by register (kids vs children vs offspring).
- Grammar: ask them to correct a sentence, or rewrite one with a target structure (passive, third conditional, reported speech).
- Paper 1 drills: give a prompt in IB style ("Your school has introduced a phone-free day. Write a blog post for students…"), ask for the opening and closing, a paragraph, or the full text; mark on criteria A (language), B (message) and C (conceptual understanding).
- Paper 2 style: a short reading text with questions of the real kinds: true/false with justification, short answers, matching, gap fills, "which word in the text means…".
- Oral practice: SL describe a visual stimulus (a photo of a protest, a crowded beach) and link it to the theme and to English-speaking cultures; HL discuss an extract from their literary work. Then ask follow-up questions on another theme.
- Feedback: show corrections inline (original → correction), name the error type (tense, agreement, article, word choice), and suggest one more sophisticated alternative.
