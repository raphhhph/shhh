# Environmental systems and societies (SL/HL)
Groups 3 and 4 (counts as either or both) · first assessment 2026 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h
Aliases: ESS, Environmental systems, ESS SL, ESS HL

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: unseen case study with data in various forms; analysis and evaluation, all compulsory | 1h, 25% | 2h, 35% |
| Paper 2: Section A short-answer and data-based; Section B structured essays with limited choice | 2h, 50% | 2h30, 45% |
| Individual investigation (IA): own research question, ≤3,000-word report | 25% | 20% |
Three key concepts: perspectives, systems, sustainability. HL adds three lenses: environmental law, environmental and ecological economics, environmental ethics. SL and HL weights here follow the 2026 subject brief; check the HL figures with the teacher.

## Topics
### 1 · Foundation
- 1.1 Perspectives: environmental value systems (ecocentric, anthropocentric, technocentric), worldviews and how they shape decisions, environmental movements, indigenous perspectives
- 1.2 Systems: open/closed/isolated systems, storages and flows, transfers vs transformations, inputs and outputs, models and their limits, feedback (positive/negative), equilibrium (steady state, static), tipping points, resilience
- 1.3 Sustainability: environmental, social and economic sustainability, natural capital and natural income, ecosystem services, ecological footprint, planetary boundaries, doughnut economics, circular economy, SDGs

### 2 · Ecology
- 2.1 Individuals, populations, communities and ecosystems: species, habitat, niche (fundamental vs realized), abiotic and biotic factors, population growth curves (J and S), carrying capacity, limiting factors, interactions (predation, herbivory, parasitism, mutualism, competition), sampling (quadrats, transects, Lincoln index, Simpson's diversity index)
- 2.2 Energy and biomass in ecosystems: photosynthesis and respiration, trophic levels, food chains and webs, pyramids of numbers/biomass/productivity, ecological efficiency (~10%), GPP, NPP, GSP, NSP calculations, bioaccumulation and biomagnification
- 2.3 Biogeochemical cycles: carbon and nitrogen cycles, storages and flows, human impacts on them
- 2.4 Climate and biomes: climate and the tricellular model, biome distribution, biome characteristics, shifts in biomes with climate change
- 2.5 Zonation, succession and change in ecosystems: zonation, primary and secondary succession, seres and climax communities, r- and K-strategists, disturbance, human impact on succession

### 3 · Biodiversity and conservation
- 3.1 Biodiversity and evolution: species, habitat and genetic diversity; natural selection; speciation; mass extinctions
- 3.2 Human impact on biodiversity: habitat loss and fragmentation, overexploitation, invasive species, pollution, climate change; IUCN Red List criteria; biodiversity hotspots
- 3.3 Conservation and regeneration: in situ and ex situ conservation, protected area design, species-based vs habitat-based approaches, rewilding, regeneration of ecosystems, international conventions (CITES, CBD)

### 4 · Water
- 4.1 Water systems: hydrological cycle, storages and flows, ocean circulation, human impact on flows
- 4.2 Water access, use and security: water scarcity, water footprints, competing demands, conflicts, strategies (desalination, rainwater harvesting, reuse)
- 4.3 Aquatic food production systems: fisheries and aquaculture, maximum sustainable yield, overfishing, sustainable management (quotas, MPAs, certification)
- 4.4 Water pollution: point and non-point sources, BOD, eutrophication, dead zones, indicator species and biotic indices, pollution management (prevention, limiting release, clean-up)

### 5 · Land
- 5.1 Soil: soil profile and systems, formation, texture triangle, fertility, degradation (erosion, salinization, desertification), conservation
- 5.2 Agriculture and food: food production systems (commercial vs subsistence, intensive vs extensive), food security and waste, diets and their footprints, sustainable agriculture (regenerative, organic, agroforestry)

### 6 · Atmosphere and climate change
- 6.1 Introduction to the atmosphere: composition, layers, the greenhouse effect as a natural process
- 6.2 Climate change, causes and impacts: enhanced greenhouse effect, greenhouse gases and sources, feedback loops, tipping points, impacts on ecosystems and societies, climate justice
- 6.3 Climate change, mitigation and adaptation: reducing emissions, carbon sinks and geoengineering, adaptation strategies, international agreements (Kyoto, Paris), carbon pricing
- 6.4 Stratospheric ozone: ozone formation and destruction, ODSs (CFCs), the ozone hole, the Montreal Protocol

### 7 · Natural resources
- 7.1 Natural resources, uses and management: renewable vs non-renewable, resource security, valuing resources
- 7.2 Energy sources, uses and management: fossil fuels, nuclear, renewables (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, biomass), energy security and choices
- 7.3 Solid waste: types and sources, waste management strategies (reduce, reuse, recycle, compost, landfill, incineration), circular economy

### 8 · Human populations and urban systems
- 8.1 Human population dynamics: birth, death, fertility rates, doubling time, demographic transition model, population pyramids, population policies
- 8.2 Urban systems and urban planning: urbanization, urban ecosystems, urban sprawl, sustainable cities, green infrastructure
- 8.3 Urban air pollution: primary and secondary pollutants, photochemical smog, temperature inversions, health impacts, management

## How to quiz it
- Definitions and "outline" questions (1–3 marks), calculations (NPP, GPP, Lincoln index, Simpson's index, doubling time = 70 / growth %, ecological efficiency).
- Diagrams to describe: systems diagrams with storages and flows, feedback loops, population curves, pyramids.
- Case-study style (Paper 1): a short invented place with data (a coral reef in Hainan, a city's air quality data); ask to analyse and evaluate management strategies from different value systems.
- Essays (Paper 2 Section B): "To what extent…" or "Evaluate…" (9 marks or more): need perspectives, systems thinking and a justified conclusion.
