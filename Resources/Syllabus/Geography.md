# Geography (SL/HL)
Group 3 (Individuals and societies) · first assessment 2019 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h
Aliases: Geography, Geog, Geography HL, Geography SL

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: geographic themes, two options at SL (three at HL); structured questions plus an extended answer per option | 1h30, 35% | 2h15, 35% |
| Paper 2: geographic perspectives, global change (core): short answers, a data/infographic question, an extended answer | 1h15, 40% | 1h15, 25% |
| Paper 3: geographic perspectives, global interactions (HL extension): one essay from a choice | n/a | 1h, 20% |
| Internal assessment: fieldwork report on a question they collected primary data for, 2500 words | 25% | 20% |
Concepts used everywhere: place, process, power, possibility, plus scale, spatial interactions and sustainability. Every answer needs located, real examples and case studies.

## Core (SL and HL): Geographic perspectives, global change
- 1 Population distribution, changing population: population distribution and density, megacities, demographic transition model, population pyramids, dependency ratios, ageing and youthful populations, forced and voluntary migration, pro- and anti-natalist policies, gender and population
- 2 Global climate, vulnerability and resilience: the energy balance and greenhouse effect, causes of climate change (natural and human), impacts (sea level, extreme weather, food, water, health, ecosystems), vulnerability by place and group, mitigation (carbon capture, renewables, reforestation, global agreements) and adaptation, carbon footprints
- 3 Global resource consumption and security: water, food and energy security, the water–food–energy nexus, measuring development (GDP, HDI, ecological footprint), resource stocks and consumption trends, circular economy, recycling, sustainable development goals

## Options (Paper 1; SL two, HL three)
- A Freshwater: drainage basins and hydrology, flooding and flood management, water scarcity and quality, dams and basin management, internationally shared rivers
- B Oceans and coastal margins: ocean currents and the conveyor belt, El Niño and La Niña, coastal processes and landforms, coastal management (hard and soft engineering), ocean resources, EEZs and sovereignty
- C Extreme environments: cold and high-altitude, arid environments, glacial and periglacial processes, desertification, opportunities and challenges, tourism and resource extraction
- D Geophysical hazards: plate tectonics, volcanoes, earthquakes, mass movement, hazard risk and vulnerability, prediction, preparedness, response and recovery
- E Leisure, tourism and sport: growth of tourism, national and international tourism, the tourism life cycle (Butler), sport and its geography, sustainability and ecotourism
- F Food and health: food systems and the green revolution, famine and food security, diseases (infectious and non-communicable), the epidemiological transition, health and development
- G Urban environments: urbanization, suburbanization, counter-urbanization, urban land use models, megacities, urban stresses (crime, congestion, pollution, informal settlements), sustainable and smart cities

## HL extension: Global interactions (Paper 3)
- Power, places and networks: globalization, global superpowers and emerging powers, TNCs, global trade and financial flows, the role of the internet
- Human development and diversity: development opportunities, cultural diffusion and homogenization, identity and resistance
- Global risks and resilience: geopolitical, economic and environmental risks, the trade-off between global interaction and local resilience

## How to quiz it
- Paper 1 style: "Describe the pattern shown in the map" [3], "Explain two reasons…" [4], "Explain how…" [6], then a 10-mark extended answer ("Examine…", "Evaluate…", "To what extent…") marked with bands.
- Paper 2: quick data questions from a graph or table (read values, calculate change), then "Explain…" and an extended "Discuss…".
- Paper 3 (HL): a 12–16-mark essay with a clear argument, scale and perspectives considered, and a conclusion that answers the question.
- Always ask for named, located examples and case studies (a city, a river, a country, a company); answers without them can't reach top bands.
- Common slips to probe: describing a pattern without data from the figure, mixing up mitigation and adaptation, causes vs consequences, one-scale answers (no local vs global), generic examples ("in Africa").
