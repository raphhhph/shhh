# Global Politics (SL/HL)
Group 3 (Individuals and societies) · updated guide, first assessment 2026 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h
Aliases: Global Politics, GloPo, GP HL, GP SL
Assessment details below are a summary in our words and unverified: check paper formats and marks with the teacher.

## How it's structured
- Core: four thematic studies, each built on key concepts: power, sovereignty and international relations; human rights; development; peace and conflict.
- Key concepts: power, sovereignty, legitimacy, interdependence, human rights, justice, liberty, equality, development, globalization, inequality, sustainability, peace, conflict, violence, non-violence.
- Assessment (outline): Paper 1 on a set of short stimulus sources (analyse, compare, evaluate); Paper 2 essays on the core themes; at HL, the HL extension on global political challenges (case studies presented orally); an engagement activity (IA): a written report on a political issue they engaged with in person, linked to theory.

## Core topics
- Power, sovereignty and international relations: types of power (hard, soft, smart, structural), state sovereignty and its limits, legitimacy, state and non-state actors (IGOs such as the UN, NGOs, MNCs, social movements, armed groups), the UN Security Council and veto, international law, theories (realism, liberalism, constructivism, Marxism, feminism, postcolonialism)
- Human rights: the UDHR and later treaties, generations of rights, universality vs cultural relativism, enforcement (ICC, regional courts, NGOs such as Amnesty International), humanitarian intervention and R2P, human rights in practice (case studies)
- Development: measures (GDP, HDI, MPI, GII, happiness indices), theories (modernization, dependency, neoliberalism, sustainable development, post-development), factors that help or hinder, the SDGs, aid, trade, FDI, debt, the role of the IMF, World Bank and WTO
- Peace and conflict: types of conflict (interstate, intrastate, ethnic, resource, terrorism), causes, negative vs positive peace (Galtung), structural and cultural violence, conflict resolution (negotiation, mediation, peacekeeping, peacebuilding), post-conflict transformation and justice

## HL extension: global political challenges (case studies)
Environment, poverty, health, identity, borders, security, technology, equality: each studied through a real case, with the interplay of actors, concepts and scales.

## How to quiz it
- Paper 1 style: a short source (a speech extract, a cartoon, a data table); ask "Identify…" [3], "Explain, with reference to the source…" [4], "Compare and contrast…" [8], "Evaluate the claim that…, using the sources and your knowledge" [9].
- Essays: "Discuss…", "Evaluate…", "To what extent…": a clear argument, key concepts defined and used, real contemporary examples (named states, organizations, conflicts), counter-arguments, a justified conclusion.
- Theory drills: ask how a realist and a liberal would each explain a current event.
- Common slips to probe: describing events without using concepts, treating sovereignty as absolute, only state actors, dated or vague examples, no counter-argument.
