# History (SL/HL)
Group 3 (Individuals and societies) · first assessment 2017 guide (check with the teacher if the school has moved to a newer one) · SL 150 h, HL 240 h
Aliases: History, History HL, History SL, Hist

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: source-based, one prescribed subject; four sources, four questions, 24 marks | 1h, 30% | 1h, 20% |
| Paper 2: two essays on two different world history topics, 15 marks each | 1h30, 45% | 1h30, 25% |
| Paper 3: three essays on one HL regional option, 15 marks each (HL only) | n/a | 2h30, 35% |
| Internal assessment: historical investigation, about 2200 words: identification and evaluation of sources, the investigation, reflection | 25% | 20% |
Key concepts: change, continuity, causation, consequence, significance, perspectives.

## Paper 1 · Prescribed subjects (the school picks one; each has two case studies)
- PS1 Military leaders: Genghis Khan (c. 1200–1227, rise, campaigns, impact); Richard I of England (1173–1199, Crusades, leadership)
- PS2 Conquest and its impact: the final stages of Muslim rule in Spain (1482–1492); the conquest of Mexico and Peru (1519–1551)
- PS3 The move to global war: Japanese expansion in East Asia (1931–1941: Manchuria, the League of Nations, the Second Sino-Japanese War, Pearl Harbor); German and Italian expansion (1933–1940: Abyssinia, Rhineland, Anschluss, Munich, appeasement, the Nazi–Soviet Pact)
- PS4 Rights and protest: the civil rights movement in the United States (1954–1965: Brown v. Board, Montgomery, Little Rock, sit-ins, Freedom Rides, Birmingham, March on Washington, Civil Rights Act, Selma, Voting Rights Act); apartheid South Africa (1948–1964: legislation, ANC and PAC, Defiance Campaign, Freedom Charter, Sharpeville, Rivonia trial)
- PS5 Conflict and intervention: Rwanda (1990–1998: causes of the genocide, the role of the UN and France, the RPF, aftermath); Kosovo (1989–2002: Milošević, KLA, Račak, NATO bombing, UN administration)
Paper 1 questions: Q1 (a) what does Source A say [3], (b) what is the message of the visual source [2]; Q2 value and limitations of a source by its origin, purpose and content (OPVL) [4]; Q3 compare and contrast two sources [6]; Q4 "using the sources and your own knowledge" essay-style answer [9].

## Paper 2 · World history topics (the school picks two; an essay on each)
- WH1 Society and economy (750–1400)
- WH2 Causes and effects of medieval wars (750–1500)
- WH3 Dynasties and rulers (750–1500)
- WH4 Societies in transition (1400–1700)
- WH5 Early modern states (1450–1789)
- WH6 Causes and effects of early modern wars (1500–1750)
- WH7 Origins, development and impact of industrialization (1750–2005)
- WH8 Independence movements (1800–2000)
- WH9 Evolution and development of democratic states (1848–2000)
- WH10 Authoritarian states (20th century): emergence (conditions, methods), consolidation and maintenance of power (propaganda, terror, legal methods), aims and results of policies (economic, social, cultural, treatment of minorities, women); typical case studies Hitler, Mussolini, Stalin, Mao, Castro, Nasser, Perón
- WH11 Causes and effects of 20th-century wars: types of war (civil, guerrilla, limited, total), causes (economic, ideological, political, territorial), practices (technology, home front, resistance), effects (peace settlements, political repercussions, social and demographic impact); typical cases WWI, WWII, Spanish Civil War, Chinese Civil War, Vietnam, Falklands, Iran–Iraq
- WH12 The Cold War: superpower tensions and rivalries (20th century): rivalry, mistrust and accord (Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, Berlin, Korea, Cuba, détente), leaders and nations (two leaders from different regions), Cold War crises (two from different regions), the end of the Cold War (Gorbachev, Eastern Europe 1989, collapse of the USSR)

## Paper 3 · HL regional options (one region, 18 sections each; the school teaches about three)
- Africa and the Middle East · the Americas · Asia and Oceania · Europe. Example sections: Europe "Imperial Russia, revolution and the establishment of the Soviet Union (1855–1924)", "Interwar domestic developments in European states (1918–1939)", "Diplomacy in Europe (1945–2000)"; Asia and Oceania "China and Korea (1910–1950)", "Japan (1912–1990)"; the Americas "The Great Depression and the Americas (mid 1920s–1939)", "Civil rights and social movements in the Americas post-1945".

## Essay skills that get marked (Papers 2 and 3, 15 marks)
- Answer the question set: focus on its command term ("to what extent", "compare and contrast", "evaluate", "discuss", "examine") and its dates.
- Structure: an introduction with a clear argument, analytical paragraphs led by a point (not narrative), accurate and relevant detail (names, dates, statistics), a conclusion that answers the question.
- Top band (13–15): well structured, consistently focused, accurate detail, sustained critical analysis, evaluation of different perspectives (historians named where useful), a reasoned conclusion. 10–12: focused, mostly analytical, some perspectives. 7–9: some analysis but parts are narrative. Lower: mostly description, little focus.
- Historiography is credit-worthy but not required; it must be used, not just name-dropped (e.g. intentionalist vs structuralist on Hitler, orthodox vs revisionist on the Cold War).

## How to quiz it
- Ask which prescribed subject and world history topics their school does (or check their notes) before going deep; default to PS3 or PS4, WH10, WH11 and WH12, the most common choices.
- Paper 1 drills: give a short invented but realistic source with its origin (author, date, type, audience); ask for OPVL [4], what it says [3], or compare two sources [6]. Never pretend an invented source is real; say it's a practice source.
- Paper 2 and 3: give a real-style essay question ("Evaluate the importance of propaganda in the consolidation of power of one authoritarian state."); ask for a thesis and a paragraph plan, or a full paragraph, and mark against the bands.
- Factual recall: dates, names and events for their case studies; causes and consequences ranked by importance.
- Feedback: say where it slips into narrative, which evidence would strengthen it, and how to push it up a band.
