# IB Middle Years Programme (MYP)
Ages 11–16, years 1–5. Every subject is assessed against four criteria, A to D, each out of 8.
Aliases: MYP, Middle Years

## Criteria by subject group (each 0–8)
| Subject group | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Language and literature | Analysing | Organizing | Producing text | Using language |
| Language acquisition | Listening | Reading | Speaking | Writing |
| Individuals and societies | Knowing and understanding | Investigating | Communicating | Thinking critically |
| Sciences | Knowing and understanding | Inquiring and designing | Processing and evaluating | Reflecting on the impacts of science |
| Mathematics | Knowing and understanding | Investigating patterns | Communicating | Applying mathematics in real-life contexts |
| Arts | Investigating | Developing | Creating/performing | Evaluating |
| Physical and health education | Knowing and understanding | Planning for performance | Applying and performing | Reflecting and improving performance |
| Design | Inquiring and analysing | Developing ideas | Creating the solution | Evaluating |

## What each criterion asks for, by subject group (year 5 strands, in our words)
- Sciences: A explain scientific knowledge, apply it to familiar and unfamiliar situations, analyse and evaluate information to make judgements. B explain a problem, a testable hypothesis with scientific reasoning, how to manipulate variables and collect data, and design a safe, complete method. C present data in transformed forms (tables, graphs), interpret it with scientific reasoning, evaluate the hypothesis and the method, explain improvements. D explain how science addresses a problem, discuss and evaluate its implications (moral, ethical, social, economic, political, cultural, environmental), use scientific language and document sources.
- Mathematics: A select and apply mathematical problem-solving techniques, solve problems correctly in a variety of contexts. B select and apply strategies to find patterns, describe them as general rules, prove or verify and justify them. C use correct mathematical language and representations (formulae, diagrams, tables, graphs), move between them, communicate a complete, coherent line of reasoning. D identify relevant elements of real-life situations, select strategies, apply them to reach a valid solution, justify the accuracy and degree to which the solution makes sense in context.
- Individuals and societies: A use terminology, demonstrate knowledge and understanding through descriptions, explanations and examples. B formulate a research question and justify it, a research action plan, collect and record information, evaluate the process and results. C communicate in a clear, organized way and document sources. D discuss concepts, issues and theories, synthesize information to make a valid argument, analyse and evaluate sources (origin, purpose, value, limitations), interpret different perspectives and their implications.
- Language and literature: A analyse content, context, language, structure, technique and style and their effect on the reader; justify opinions with examples; compare and contrast texts. B organize ideas (structure, transitions), use referencing and formatting. C produce texts with insight and imagination, make stylistic choices aware of the audience, select relevant details and examples. D use a varied range of vocabulary and sentence structures, the right register and style, correct grammar, spelling and punctuation, clear pronunciation and intonation when spoken.
- Language acquisition (phases 1 to 6): A listening and B reading: identify explicit and implicit information, analyse conventions, connections and the author's purpose. C speaking: use vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation accurately, interact and communicate ideas and opinions in a range of situations. D writing: use vocabulary and grammatical structures, organize information with cohesive devices, communicate information in the right register and text type. The expected depth rises with the phase, not the year.
- Design: A explain and justify the need and the brief, research, analyse existing products, a design specification. B a range of feasible design ideas, select one and justify it, planning drawings or diagrams. C a logical plan (time, resources), demonstrate technical skills, follow the plan, justify changes. D design testing methods, evaluate the solution against the specification, explain how it could be improved, its impact on the client or target audience.
- Arts and PHE follow the same pattern (A knowledge or investigating, B developing or planning, C creating or performing, D evaluating or reflecting), with the process journal or portfolio as evidence.

## Levels
Each criterion has four bands: 1–2 (limited), 3–4 (adequate), 5–6 (substantial), 7–8 (excellent). 0 = nothing that reaches level 1.
The best-fit band is chosen from the descriptor, then the higher or lower mark within it.

## Final grade (1–7) from the total of the four criteria (out of 32)
1–5 → 1 · 6–9 → 2 · 10–14 → 3 · 15–18 → 4 · 19–23 → 5 · 24–27 → 6 · 28–32 → 7

## Framework
- Global contexts: identities and relationships; orientation in space and time; personal and cultural expression; scientific and technical innovation; globalization and sustainability; fairness and development.
- Key concepts (16): aesthetics, change, communication, communities, connections, creativity, culture, development, form, global interactions, identity, logic, perspective, relationships, systems, time place and space.
- ATL skills: communication, social, self-management, research, thinking.
- Personal Project (MYP 5): criterion A Planning, B Applying skills, C Reflecting (each 0–8); a product plus a report.
- MYP 5 eAssessment: on-screen exams in language and literature, individuals and societies, sciences, mathematics (standard or extended), and an interdisciplinary exam; ePortfolios for language acquisition, arts, PHE, design.

## How to quiz MYP students
- Say which criterion a question targets ("Criterion A: Knowing and understanding").
- Mark with the band, the mark out of 8 and why, using the descriptor's language: "states" (1–2), "outlines" (3–4), "describes" (5–6), "explains" (7–8) for knowing and understanding.
- Questions fit the year: MYP 1–3 simpler contexts, MYP 4–5 closer to eAssessment style, with real-world contexts and a global context named.
