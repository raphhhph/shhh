## Topics (SL = everyone; AHL = HL only)
### 1 · Number and algebra
- 1.1 Scientific notation (a × 10^k), operations with it
- 1.2 Arithmetic sequences and series: u_n, S_n, sigma notation, applications
- 1.3 Geometric sequences and series: u_n, S_n, applications (growth, decay)
- 1.4 Financial applications: compound interest, annual depreciation
- 1.5 Laws of exponents with integer exponents; introduction to logarithms (log₁₀ and ln)
- 1.6 Simple deductive proof, equality vs identity (LHS to RHS)
- 1.7 Laws of exponents with rational exponents; laws of logarithms, change of base; solving exponential equations
- 1.8 Sum of an infinite geometric series (|r| < 1)
- 1.9 Binomial theorem for (a + b)^n, n ∈ ℕ; Pascal's triangle; nCr
- AHL 1.10 Counting principles: permutations and combinations; binomial theorem extended to rational exponents (with |x| conditions)
- AHL 1.11 Partial fractions (up to two distinct linear factors in the denominator)
- AHL 1.12 Complex numbers: i, Cartesian form z = a + bi, real and imaginary parts, conjugate, modulus, argument, the complex plane
- AHL 1.13 Modulus–argument (polar) form r cis θ and Euler form re^{iθ}; sums, products and quotients in each form; geometric interpretation
- AHL 1.14 Complex conjugate roots of polynomials with real coefficients; De Moivre's theorem and its proof by induction; powers and nth roots of complex numbers
- AHL 1.15 Proof by mathematical induction, by contradiction, and disproof by counterexample
- AHL 1.16 Solving systems of linear equations (up to three unknowns), unique, no or infinitely many solutions

### 2 · Functions
- 2.1 Equations of straight lines: gradient, intercepts, parallel and perpendicular lines
- 2.2 Concept of a function, domain, range, graph; inverse function as a reflection in y = x
- 2.3 Graphs of functions, by hand and with technology
- 2.4 Key features of graphs: max and min, intercepts, symmetry, vertex, zeros, asymptotes; intersecting curves
- 2.5 Composite functions (f ∘ g)(x), identity function, finding f⁻¹(x)
- 2.6 Quadratic functions: y = ax² + bx + c, vertex form a(x − h)² + k, factored form a(x − p)(x − q); axis of symmetry
- 2.7 Solving quadratic equations and inequalities; the quadratic formula; the discriminant Δ and the nature of roots
- 2.8 Reciprocal function 1/x and rational functions (ax + b)/(cx + d): asymptotes and graphs
- 2.9 Exponential functions a^x and e^x, logarithmic functions log_a x and ln x, and their graphs
- 2.10 Solving equations analytically and graphically, including in context
- 2.11 Transformations of graphs: translations, vertical and horizontal stretches, reflections in the axes, composite transformations
- AHL 2.12 Polynomial functions: graphs, zeros, the factor and remainder theorems, sum and product of roots
- AHL 2.13 Rational functions of the form (ax + b)/(cx² + dx + e) and (ax² + bx + c)/(dx + e): asymptotes including oblique
- AHL 2.14 Odd and even functions; finding the inverse when the domain is restricted; self-inverse functions
- AHL 2.15 Solving polynomial and other inequalities, graphically and analytically
- AHL 2.16 Graphs of y = |f(x)|, y = f(|x|), y = 1/f(x), y = f(ax + b), y = [f(x)]²

### 3 · Geometry and trigonometry
- 3.1 Distance and midpoint in 3D; volume and surface area of 3D solids; angle between two lines and between a line and a plane
- 3.2 Right-angled trigonometry; sine rule (including the ambiguous case), cosine rule; area = ½ab sin C

- 3.4 The circle: radian measure, arc length, sector area
- 3.5 The unit circle; exact values of sin, cos and tan for 0, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2 and their multiples; extension to negative angles

- AHL 3.9 Reciprocal trig ratios sec, csc, cot; Pythagorean identities 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ; inverse functions arcsin, arccos, arctan and their graphs

- AHL 3.11 Symmetry properties of trig graphs, relationships between them
- AHL 3.12 Vectors: geometric and component representation, position vectors, displacement, magnitude, unit vectors, base vectors i, j, k

- AHL 3.14 Vector equation of a line r = a + λb in 2D and 3D; angle between lines; kinematics of objects moving along lines
- AHL 3.15 Classifying lines as coincident, parallel, intersecting or skew; points of intersection
- AHL 3.16 Vector product: properties, area of a parallelogram and triangle
- AHL 3.17 Vector equations of a plane r = a + λb + μc and r · n = a · n; Cartesian equation ax + by + cz = d

### 4 · Statistics and probability

- 4.9 Normal distribution: properties, diagrams, calculating probabilities and inverse normal with the GDC
- 4.10 Regression line of x on y; using equations of regression lines for prediction
- 4.11 Formal definition and use of the formulae for conditional probability and independence
- 4.12 Standardization of normal variables (z-values); inverse normal with unknown mean or standard deviation
- AHL 4.13 Bayes' theorem (up to three events)

## How to quiz it
- Paper 1 style (no calculator): exact values, algebraic manipulation, a short proof or a "show that", 4–8 marks; answers like 2√3, ln 5 or 3π/4, never decimals.
- Paper 2 style (GDC): a context or a function to analyse in parts; probability distributions, intersections, numerical integration; answers to 3 s.f.
- Paper 3 style (HL): a long guided investigation that builds a general result from special cases (e.g. a family of curves, a sequence defined by a recurrence) and ends with a proof or a generalization.
- Mark with M (method), A (accuracy), R (reasoning) and AG (answer given: the result is shown, so the working is what earns the marks); follow-through when an early slip is carried correctly.
- Write maths in LaTeX on the whiteboard so it's typeset; still give final answers in their simplest exact form.
- Common slips to probe: forgetting + C, dropping the chain rule's inner derivative, radians vs degrees, domain restrictions for inverses and logs, losing solutions when dividing by sin x, the ambiguous case of the sine rule, sign errors in integration by parts, induction proofs that skip stating the assumption for n = k.
