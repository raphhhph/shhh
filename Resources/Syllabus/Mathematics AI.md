Group 5 · first assessment 2021 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h · GDC allowed on every paper
Aliases: Math AI, Maths AI, Math A&I, AI HL, AI SL

| Component | SL | HL |

## Topics (SL = everyone; AHL = HL only)
### 1 · Number and algebra
- 1.1 Standard form (a × 10^k), operations with it
- 1.2 Arithmetic sequences and series: u_n, S_n, real-life uses (simple interest, seating rows)
- 1.3 Geometric sequences and series: u_n, S_n, growth/decay, population and depreciation
- 1.4 Financial applications: compound interest, annual depreciation, using the GDC's TVM solver (N, I%, PV, PMT, FV, P/Y, C/Y)
- 1.5 Exponents and logarithms: laws of exponents, log as inverse, solving a^x = b with logs
- 1.6 Approximation: decimal places, s.f., upper/lower bounds, percentage error
- 1.7 Amortization and annuities with the TVM solver
- 1.8 Solving systems of linear equations and polynomial equations with the GDC
- AHL 1.9 Laws of logarithms (any base), change of base
- AHL 1.10 Simplifying expressions with rational exponents
- AHL 1.11 Sum of an infinite geometric series (|r| < 1)
- AHL 1.12 Complex numbers: i, Cartesian form a + bi, complex plane (Argand diagram), operations
- AHL 1.13 Modulus–argument (polar) form r(cos θ + i sin θ) = r cis θ and Euler form re^{iθ}; products, quotients, powers; use in sinusoidal/AC problems
- AHL 1.14 Matrices: order, operations, identity, determinant, inverse (2×2 by hand, larger by GDC), solving systems with matrices
- AHL 1.15 Eigenvalues and eigenvectors (2×2), characteristic polynomial, diagonalization, powers of matrices A^n = PD^nP⁻¹

### 2 · Functions
- 2.1 Equations of lines: y = mx + c, ax + by + d = 0, point–gradient form; parallel and perpendicular lines
- 2.2 Functions: domain, range, mapping, inverse as reflection in y = x, graph sketching vs drawing
- 2.3 Graphing with technology
- 2.4 Key features: max/min, intercepts, symmetry, vertex, zeros, asymptotes; intersection of two curves on the GDC
- 2.5 Modelling with linear, quadratic, exponential (a·b^x + c, a·e^{kx} + c), direct/inverse variation, cubic and sinusoidal (a sin(bx) + d) functions
- 2.6 The modelling cycle: choose a model, fit parameters, test, reflect on assumptions and domain
- AHL 2.7 Composite functions, inverse of a function, finding inverses algebraically
- AHL 2.8 Transformations of graphs: translations, stretches, reflections (and composite transformations)
- AHL 2.9 Further modelling: exponential (incl. natural), logarithmic, logistic L/(1 + Ce^{-kx}), piecewise; sinusoidal with phase shift
- AHL 2.10 Scaling very large/small data with logs; linearizing with semi-log (y = ab^x) and log–log (y = ax^b) graphs

### 3 · Geometry and trigonometry
- 3.1 3D shapes: distance and midpoint in 3D, volume and surface area of pyramids, cones, spheres, hemispheres, prisms; angle between a line and a plane
- 3.2 Right-angled trig (SOHCAHTOA), sine rule, cosine rule, area = ½ab sin C
- 3.3 Applications: angles of elevation and depression, bearings, constructing labelled diagrams from words
- 3.4 Circles: arc length and sector area in degrees
- 3.5 Equations of perpendicular bisectors
- 3.6 Voronoi diagrams: sites, vertices, edges, cells; adding a site; nearest-neighbour interpolation; "toxic waste dump" problem
- AHL 3.7 Radians, arc length and sector area in radians
- AHL 3.8 Unit circle, sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, tan θ = sin θ / cos θ, solving trig equations graphically
- AHL 3.9 Geometric transformations with matrices: reflections, rotations, enlargements, stretches; composition; area scale factor = |det|
- AHL 3.10 Vectors: notation, components, magnitude, unit vectors, position vectors, displacement
- AHL 3.11 Vector equation of a line r = a + λb; angle between lines
- AHL 3.12 Vector kinematics: position as a function of time, velocity, closest approach, meeting
- AHL 3.13 Scalar (dot) product and vector (cross) product; angle between vectors; area of a parallelogram/triangle
- AHL 3.14 Graph theory: vertices, edges, degree, simple/complete/connected/weighted/directed graphs, subgraphs, trees
- AHL 3.15 Adjacency matrices, walks of length k (A^k), weighted adjacency tables, transition matrices of graphs
- AHL 3.16 Algorithms: minimum spanning tree (Kruskal, Prim), Chinese postman (route inspection), travelling salesman (nearest-neighbour upper bound, deleted-vertex lower bound); Eulerian and Hamiltonian paths and cycles

### 4 · Statistics and probability
- 4.1 Population vs sample, discrete vs continuous data, sampling methods (simple random, convenience, systematic, quota, stratified), bias, outliers
- 4.2 Presenting data: frequency tables, histograms, cumulative frequency graphs (median, quartiles, percentiles), box-and-whisker plots
- 4.3 Measures of centre and spread: mean, median, mode, range, IQR, standard deviation; effect of constant changes
- 4.4 Linear correlation: Pearson's r, scatter diagrams, line of best fit through the mean point, regression line y on x and its use (interpolation vs extrapolation)
- 4.5 Probability: sample space, events, complementary events, expected frequency
- 4.6 Venn diagrams, tree diagrams, tables; combined, mutually exclusive, conditional, independent events
- 4.7 Discrete random variables, probability distributions, expected value, fair games
- 4.8 Binomial distribution B(n, p): when it applies, mean np, variance np(1 − p)
- 4.9 Normal distribution: properties, probabilities and inverse normal on the GDC
- 4.10 Spearman's rank correlation; when to use it instead of Pearson's
- 4.11 Hypothesis tests: null and alternative hypotheses, significance level, p-value; χ² test for independence (contingency tables, degrees of freedom, expected frequencies ≥ 5) and goodness of fit; t-test (equal variance, one- and two-tailed)
- AHL 4.12 Designing data collection: reliability (test–retest, parallel forms) and validity (content, criterion); choosing relevant variables
- AHL 4.13 Non-linear regression (quadratic, cubic, exponential, power, sine), sum of squared residuals, coefficient of determination R²
- AHL 4.14 Linear transformations of a random variable: E(aX + b), Var(aX + b); unbiased estimates of mean and variance
- AHL 4.15 Linear combinations of independent normal variables; the central limit theorem; distribution of the sample mean
- AHL 4.16 Confidence intervals for the mean (normal and t), interpreting them
- AHL 4.17 Poisson distribution Po(m): conditions, mean = variance, sums of Poisson variables
- AHL 4.18 Critical values and regions, type I and type II errors; tests for a population mean, proportion (binomial), Poisson mean, and correlation (Pearson's r)
- AHL 4.19 Transition matrices and Markov chains: state diagrams, n-step probabilities, steady state (long-term) probabilities

### 5 · Calculus
- 5.1 Derivative as gradient function and rate of change; limits informally
- 5.2 Increasing and decreasing functions; graphical meaning of f′(x) > 0, < 0
- 5.3 Differentiating ax^n (n integer)
- 5.4 Tangents and normals at a point
- 5.5 Integration as anti-differentiation of ax^n (n ≠ −1); definite integrals with the GDC; area between a curve and the x-axis
- 5.6 Stationary points, local max and min
- 5.7 Optimisation in context (area, volume, profit, cost)
- 5.8 Trapezoidal rule for estimating areas from functions or data
- AHL 5.9 Derivatives of sin x, cos x, tan x, e^x, ln x, x^n (rational n); chain, product and quotient rules; related rates of change
- AHL 5.10 Second derivative; concavity, points of inflection
- AHL 5.11 Indefinite integrals of those functions, integration by inspection and substitution
- AHL 5.12 Area between a curve and the y-axis; volumes of revolution about the x- and y-axes
- AHL 5.13 Kinematics: displacement, velocity, acceleration, total distance travelled
- AHL 5.14 Setting up differential equations from context; solving by separation of variables
- AHL 5.15 Slope fields and what they show
- AHL 5.16 Euler's method for first-order differential equations and coupled systems
- AHL 5.17 Phase portraits for coupled linear systems dx/dt = ax + by, dy/dt = cx + dy using eigenvalues (real distinct, complex, imaginary); equilibrium points, stability, sketching trajectories
- AHL 5.18 Second-order differential equations solved numerically with Euler's method (rewritten as a coupled system)

## How to quiz it
- Numbers they can do with a GDC: give real-looking contexts (loans, populations, bacteria, phone plans, routes between towns).
- Paper 1 style: 4–7 marks, one skill. Paper 2 style: a multi-part context building from (a) to (e). Paper 3 style (HL): a long guided investigation, e.g. modelling a disease with a logistic model, or Markov chains for market share.
- Mark with M (method) and A (accuracy) marks; follow-through (FT) when an early slip is carried correctly.
- Common slips to probe: 3 s.f. rounding too early, degrees vs radians, PMT sign in TVM, χ² expected values, Pearson vs Spearman choice, extrapolating a regression line, interpreting the p-value backwards.
