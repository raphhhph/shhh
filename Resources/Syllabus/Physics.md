# Physics (SL/HL)
Group 4 (Sciences) · first assessment 2025 · SL 150 h, HL 240 h · calculator on both papers, data booklet provided
Aliases: Physics, Physics HL, Physics SL, Phys

## How it's assessed
| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1A: multiple choice (no penalty for wrong answers) and Paper 1B: data-based questions on an experiment, sat together | 1h30, 36% | 2h, 36% |
| Paper 2: short-answer and extended-response questions across the syllabus | 1h30, 44% | 2h30, 44% |
| Internal assessment: scientific investigation of their own research question (report up to 3000 words; research design, data analysis, conclusion, evaluation) | 20% | 20% |
Also the collaborative sciences project (required, not marked). Five themes, A to E, each with topics; (HL) marks HL-only content. Answers with units and sensible significant figures; show the equation, the substitution, then the answer.

## Topics ((HL) = additional higher level)
### A · Space, time and motion
- A.1 Kinematics: distance vs displacement, speed vs velocity, acceleration; motion graphs (gradient and area); suvat equations for uniform acceleration; projectile motion (independent horizontal and vertical components); qualitative effect of fluid resistance, terminal speed
- A.2 Forces and momentum: Newton's three laws, free-body diagrams, weight, normal reaction, tension, friction (static and dynamic, μ), drag, buoyancy, Hooke's law; linear momentum p = mv, impulse FΔt = Δp, force–time graphs, conservation of momentum, elastic and inelastic collisions, explosions; circular motion (centripetal acceleration v²/r = ω²r, angular velocity, period); (HL) energy considerations in collisions and explosions
- A.3 Work, energy and power: W = Fs cos θ, kinetic, gravitational potential and elastic potential energy, conservation of energy, energy (Sankey) diagrams, power P = Fv, efficiency, energy density
- A.4 Rigid body mechanics (HL only): torque τ = Fr sin θ, rotational equilibrium, angular displacement/velocity/acceleration, moment of inertia, Newton's second law for rotation, angular momentum and its conservation, rotational kinetic energy, rolling without slipping
- A.5 Galilean and special relativity (HL only): reference frames, Galilean transformation, postulates of special relativity, Lorentz transformations, time dilation, length contraction, relativistic velocity addition, spacetime interval, simultaneity, spacetime (Minkowski) diagrams, muon decay evidence

### B · The particulate nature of matter
- B.1 Thermal energy transfers: kinetic model, temperature (kelvin) and internal energy, specific heat capacity Q = mcΔT, specific latent heat Q = mL, phase changes, conduction (thermal conductivity), convection, radiation, black-body radiation, Stefan–Boltzmann law, Wien's law, luminosity and apparent brightness
- B.2 Greenhouse effect: solar constant, albedo and emissivity, the energy balance of the Earth, greenhouse gases and molecular absorption of infrared, the enhanced greenhouse effect
- B.3 Gas laws: pressure, the mole and Avogadro's constant, ideal gas law pV = nRT, assumptions of the kinetic model, kinetic energy of molecules (3/2 kT), real vs ideal gases
- B.4 Thermodynamics (HL only): first law Q = ΔU + W, work done on or by a gas (area under p–V graph), isovolumetric, isobaric, isothermal and adiabatic changes, second law, entropy (macroscopic ΔS = ΔQ/T and microscopic S = k ln Ω), heat engines, Carnot cycle and efficiency
- B.5 Current and circuits: charge and current, potential difference, resistance and Ohm's law, resistivity, I–V characteristics, power dissipated, series and parallel circuits, potential dividers, emf and internal resistance, cells (primary and secondary), ammeters and voltmeters

### C · Wave behaviour
- C.1 Simple harmonic motion: conditions for SHM (a ∝ −x), period, amplitude, phase, energy interchange; (HL) defining equation a = −ω²x, solutions x = x₀ sin(ωt + φ), velocity and energy equations, phase angle
- C.2 Wave model: transverse and longitudinal waves, wave speed v = fλ, sound and electromagnetic waves, wave graphs (displacement–distance and displacement–time)
- C.3 Wave phenomena: wavefronts and rays, reflection, refraction and Snell's law, critical angle and total internal reflection, diffraction, superposition, interference, path difference, double-slit interference s = λD/d, (HL) single-slit diffraction, multiple-slit and diffraction gratings nλ = d sin θ
- C.4 Standing waves and resonance: formation, nodes and antinodes, harmonics on strings and in pipes (open and closed), natural frequency, forced oscillations, resonance, damping (light, critical, heavy)
- C.5 Doppler effect: moving source and moving observer for sound, Δf/f ≈ v/c for light, redshift and blueshift, (HL) the equations for sound with moving source and observer

### D · Fields
- D.1 Gravitational fields: Kepler's laws, Newton's law of gravitation, field strength g = GM/r², field lines; (HL) gravitational potential and potential energy, equipotentials, escape speed, orbital motion, orbital speed and energy, viscous drag on satellites
- D.2 Electric and magnetic fields: charge, Coulomb's law, electric field strength, field lines, magnetic field patterns (wire, solenoid, bar magnet); (HL) electric potential and potential energy, equipotentials, potential gradient
- D.3 Motion in electromagnetic fields: charged particles in uniform electric fields, force on a moving charge F = qvB sin θ, force on a current-carrying conductor F = BIL sin θ, circular paths of charges, force between parallel wires, crossed fields (velocity selector)
- D.4 Induction (HL only): magnetic flux and flux linkage, Faraday's and Lenz's laws, induced emf in a moving conductor, generators and alternating current

### E · Nuclear and quantum physics
- E.1 Structure of the atom: Geiger–Marsden–Rutherford experiment, nuclear notation, emission and absorption spectra, photons E = hf, (HL) Bohr model and energy levels of hydrogen, nuclear radius R = R₀A^(1/3), quantization of angular momentum
- E.2 Quantum physics (HL only): photoelectric effect (threshold frequency, work function, Einstein's equation), wave–particle duality, de Broglie wavelength, electron diffraction, Compton scattering
- E.3 Radioactive decay: isotopes, nuclear binding energy and mass defect, binding energy per nucleon curve, α, β⁻, β⁺ and γ decay, the neutrino, ionizing and penetrating power, half-life, background radiation, (HL) decay constant, activity A = λN, N = N₀e^(−λt), evidence for the strong force
- E.4 Fission: spontaneous and neutron-induced fission, chain reactions, critical mass, nuclear power stations (moderator, control rods, coolant, shielding), safety and waste
- E.5 Fusion and stars: conditions for fusion, stellar equilibrium (radiation pressure vs gravity), the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram, stellar evolution by mass (main sequence, red giant, white dwarf, neutron star, black hole), parallax and distances (parsec, light-year), (HL) main-sequence lifetime and luminosity–mass relation

## Skills and tools that get examined
- Uncertainties: absolute, fractional and percentage; propagating them in sums, products and powers; error bars; best-fit and worst-fit gradients; random vs systematic error.
- Graphs: linearizing a relationship (plot T² against l, ln A against t), gradient and intercept meaning.
- Orders of magnitude and estimates; SI units and prefixes; vectors (resolving components).

## How to quiz it
- Paper 1A style: four-option multiple choice, one concept or a quick calculation; give options A–D and ask for the letter and why.
- Paper 1B style: a short experiment with a data table; ask them to plot or read a gradient, find the uncertainty, and evaluate the method.
- Paper 2 style: a context in parts (a), (b), (c) mixing "state", "calculate", "explain", "show that"; "show that" answers need the working to one more significant figure than given.
- Calculations: equation, substitution with units, answer with unit and sensible s.f.; give method marks for the right equation even if the arithmetic slips; follow-through for a carried error.
- Explanations: mark the physics chain (cause, mechanism, effect) and the key word (e.g. "net force", "resultant", "in phase", "path difference of a whole number of wavelengths").
- Common slips to probe: confusing mass and weight, forgetting the direction of momentum, kelvin vs Celsius, using diameter for radius, radians in SHM, mixing up emf and terminal p.d.
