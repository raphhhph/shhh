# IB coursework criteria (Joe's summary, in our own words)

For feedback on drafts: what each criterion rewards, what the top band looks like, and the word limit. This is a summary written for Joe, not the IB's wording; the subject guide and the teacher's instructions win when they differ. Items marked (check) are the ones most likely to have changed with a new guide.

## How to give draft feedback
- Mark against the descriptors, best fit: start from the band whose description fits most of the work, then move up or down within it.
- Quote the draft as evidence. A band with no evidence is a guess.
- "What lifts it a band" is a concrete change (add an evaluation of the method's limitation on accuracy; make the RQ name the variable and the population), never rewritten text to paste in.
- Teachers may comment on one draft only (IB rule). Joe's feedback is advice, the student writes every word.
- Words over the limit are not read by the examiner; criteria that depend on the conclusion or evaluation suffer most.

## Extended Essay (all subjects): /34, 4,000 words
- A · Focus and method (6): a clear, narrow research question stated early; a topic that suits the subject; methods and sources chosen deliberately and explained.
- B · Knowledge and understanding (6): accurate subject knowledge placed in context; correct subject terms used with precision; the sources show the student understands the field.
- C · Critical thinking (12): research that is relevant and well chosen; analysis that goes beyond description; an argument built step by step toward a conclusion that answers the RQ; evaluation of the sources and methods. The biggest criterion: a descriptive essay caps here.
- D · Presentation (4): the required structure (title page, contents, page numbers, references, appendices only for raw material), consistent referencing, readable figures and tables.
- E · Engagement (6): from the three reflections (RPPF, at most 500 words in total): personal engagement, decisions made and why, setbacks and how they were handled, growth as a researcher.
- Grade bands (approximate): A 27–34, B 21–26, C 14–20, D 7–13, E 0–6. (check: a new EE guide applies from first assessment 2027; use the school's version.)

## Theory of Knowledge
### TOK essay: /10, one holistic mark, 1,600 words, one of the six prescribed titles
- What is assessed: does the essay give a clear, coherent and critical exploration of the title?
- 9–10 Excellent: sustained focus on the title, linked to areas of knowledge; arguments and counter-claims are clear, well supported by specific real examples, and their implications are explored; insightful.
- 7–8 Very good: focused, with supported arguments and counter-claims; some implications considered; examples effective.
- 5–6 Satisfactory: relevant but some drift from the title; arguments partly supported; counter-claims thin; examples generic.
- 3–4 Basic: brief or loose links to the title; descriptive; few or weak examples.
- 1–2 Rudimentary: little connection to the title; claims unsupported.
- Common lifts: answer the title's exact words (define the key terms in TOK terms); two AOKs compared, not two parallel essays; examples that are specific (named, dated), not hypothetical; evaluate each claim with a counter-claim and a conclusion that says how far.

### TOK exhibition: /10, one holistic mark, 950 words for the whole commentary
- Three objects (real, specific, ideally from the student's own life), one IA prompt from the list of 35, one core or optional theme.
- 9–10 Excellent: each object's link to the prompt is clear and well justified; the objects are specific and their real-world context is identified; reasons are supported by evidence and quotes.
- 7–8 Very good: clear links, mostly justified, specific objects.
- 5–6 Satisfactory: links stated but partly justified; some objects generic.
- 3–4 Basic: weak links, little justification; 1–2 Rudimentary.
- Common lifts: say exactly how the object answers the prompt (not just what it is); a specific object (this photo, this letter) rather than a type; the prompt quoted word for word.

## Group 1: Studies in language and literature
### Individual oral (IO): /40, 15 minutes (10 speaking + 5 teacher questions)
- A global issue explored through an extract (at most 40 lines) from each of two works or bodies of work, one from each text type taught. Students bring at most 10 bullet points on one page.
- A · Knowledge, understanding and interpretation (10): knows both extracts and works; interpretations of how the global issue appears are supported by references.
- B · Analysis and evaluation (10): analyses how the authors' choices (structure, technique, style, image and text) present the global issue; evaluates their effect.
- C · Focus and organization (10): balanced between the two works; a clear line of argument tied to the global issue throughout.
- D · Language (10): clear, accurate, varied, register suited to an oral; precise literary and linguistic terms.
- Common lifts: name the global issue as a precise claim, not a topic; for every quote, the technique and its effect; balance time between the two works; zoom out from each extract to the whole work.
- HL essay (Lang and Lit and Literature): /20, 1,200–1,500 words: A Knowledge, understanding and interpretation 5, B Analysis and evaluation 5, C Focus, organization and development 5, D Language 5.

## Group 3: Individuals and societies
### Economics: portfolio of three commentaries
- Each commentary at most 800 words, on a news article published within a year of writing, each on a different unit and a different key concept. Each /14: A Diagrams 3, B Terminology 2, C Application and analysis 3, D Key concept 3, E Evaluation 3. Plus F Rubric requirements 3 for the whole portfolio (three units, three key concepts, articles from different, appropriate sources). Total /45.
- Common lifts: a diagram fitted to the article (real numbers or labels from it); the key concept used as the lens through the whole piece, not one sentence at the end; evaluation that weighs, with stakeholders, short vs long run, and a judgment.

### Business Management: business research project (check: new guide, first assessment 2024)
- 1,800 words, a real organization, a research question through one key concept (change, creativity, ethics or sustainability), 3 to 5 supporting documents. /25: A Integration of the key concept 5, B Supporting documents 4, C Choice and application of tools and theories 4, D Analysis and evaluation 5, E Conclusions 3, F Structure 2, G Presentation 2.

### Geography: fieldwork, 2,500 words, /25
- A Fieldwork question and geographic context 3, B Methods of investigation 3, C Quality and treatment of information collected 6, D Written analysis 8, E Conclusion 2, F Evaluation 3.

### History: historical investigation, 2,200 words, /25
- A Identification and evaluation of sources 6 (two sources, origin, purpose and content against value and limitations), B Investigation 15 (a focused argument, using evidence, weighing perspectives, reaching a conclusion), C Reflection 4 (what the investigation showed about the historian's methods and their challenges).

## Group 4: Sciences (Biology, Chemistry, Physics; first assessment 2025)
### Scientific investigation: /24, 3,000 words, up to three students may share data but each writes their own report
- Research design (6): a focused research question in context; methodology appropriate and described so it could be repeated (variables, controls, safety and ethics).
- Data analysis (6): data recorded and processed clearly (uncertainties, correct units and significant figures), graphs and calculations that are correct and relevant.
- Conclusion (6): answers the RQ, justified by the analysed data, compared with accepted science.
- Evaluation (6): the methodology's strengths and weaknesses and how they affect the results (precision, accuracy, systematic and random errors), with realistic, specific improvements.
- Common lifts: an RQ that names the independent and dependent variables, their range and units; error bars and a line of best fit with its uncertainty; each weakness tied to its effect on the data and a specific fix.

### Computer Science: the solution (check: new guide, first assessment 2027)
- A product for a real client with a written report of about 2,000 words and a video. Old guide /34: A Planning 6, B Solution overview 6, C Development 12, D Functionality and extensibility 4, E Evaluation 6.

## Group 5: Mathematics (AA and AI): the exploration, /20, 12–20 pages
- A Presentation (4): organized, coherent, concise: an introduction with an aim, a body that develops it, a conclusion.
- B Mathematical communication (4): correct notation, symbols and terms; variables defined; graphs and tables labelled; no calculator notation (^, *).
- C Personal engagement (3): the student's own ideas, choices and questions throughout, not a retold textbook topic; significant and creative.
- D Reflection (3): critical reflection on results, method, limitations and what they mean, all the way through, not only at the end.
- E Use of mathematics (6): mathematics relevant to the aim, at the level of the course (SL or HL), correct, and shown with understanding; HL top bands need sophistication and rigour.
- Common lifts: a precise aim in the first paragraph; every result interpreted in context; maths at or above the course level (an exploration resting only on GCSE-level work caps E); reflection after each stage.
