# Theory of knowledge (TOK)
DP core · first assessment 2022 · 100 hours · everyone takes it
Aliases: TOK, ToK, theory of knowledge

## How it's assessed
| Component | Weight | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| TOK exhibition (internal) | 1/3 | Three objects (or images of them) linked to one of 35 IA prompts, with a commentary of up to 950 words in total, each object with its real-world context |
| TOK essay (external) | 2/3 | 1,600 words on one of six prescribed titles released for the session |
Combined with the extended essay for up to three DP core points.

## Structure
- Core theme: knowledge and the knower (how I know, who I am, my communities, biases, perspectives)
- Optional themes (two are studied): knowledge and technology, knowledge and language, knowledge and politics, knowledge and religion, knowledge and indigenous societies
- Areas of knowledge (all five): history, the human sciences, the natural sciences, mathematics, the arts
- Knowledge framework for every theme and AOK: scope, perspectives, methods and tools, ethics

## Twelve course concepts
Evidence, certainty, truth, interpretation, power, justification, explanation, objectivity, perspective, culture, values, responsibility.

## Key ideas to know
- Knowledge questions (KQs): open, contestable, about knowledge itself, using TOK vocabulary; not about a subject's content ("To what extent does the language we use shape what we can know?" not "Is English hard to learn?")
- Real-life situations (RLS) and examples: specific, from different AOKs and from their own subjects
- Claims and counterclaims, implications, the shared vs personal knowledge distinction, reliability of sources, confirmation bias, paradigms (Kuhn), falsifiability (Popper), induction vs deduction, the problem of induction, models in the sciences, reliability of memory and testimony in history, the role of axioms in mathematics, the role of emotion and imagination in the arts, labels and classification (do labels reveal or create knowledge?)

## The 35 exhibition prompts (paraphrased themes)
What counts as knowledge; seeking certainty; knowledge and responsibility; trust; knowledge and power; changing beliefs; perspectives and bias; the role of experts; ethics in pursuit of knowledge; technology and knowledge; language and naming; tradition; uncertainty. (Read the exact list from their notes or the TOK guide before setting exhibition practice.)

## How to quiz it
- Ask them to turn a real situation into a strong knowledge question, then give feedback on how TOK it is.
- Give a KQ and ask for a claim, a counterclaim, and an example from two different AOKs.
- Concept checks: "Explain the difference between justification and evidence", "What makes a model useful even when it's false?"
- Exhibition practice: give a prompt and an object, ask them to explain the link in about 100 words, then critique it (specific, justified, linked to the prompt).
- Essay practice: give a past-style title and ask for the thesis, two perspectives and the AOKs they'd use.
